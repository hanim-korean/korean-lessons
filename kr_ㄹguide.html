A complete guide for English-speaking learners — with diagrams, tricks, and minimal pairs.

For Hanim's Students

01L vs. ㄹ vs. R — The Three Sounds Compared

Korean ㄹ is not exactly L. Not exactly R. It's a quick tap sound — your tongue touches and bounces off.

English L
L
like "lemon", "love"

Tongue tip presses firmly against the back of your front teeth — and stays there.
혀끝이 윗니 뒤에 꾹 눌러서 닿아요 — 가만히 있어요.

Korean ㄹ
ㄹ
our target sound

Tongue tip quickly taps the bumpy ridge just behind your teeth — then immediately bounces away.
혀끝으로 잇몸 뒤쪽을 살짝 대고 튕겨요 — touch and bounce!

English R
R
like "radio", "right"

Tongue is curled back in the middle of the mouth, never touching the roof. No tap.
혀를 말아서 입천장 뒤쪽에 닿아요 — 닿지 않고 떠 있어요.

💡 Tutor Note The single biggest fix for Western learners: don't hold the tongue. ㄹ is not a sustained sound. It's a flick — touch, bounce, gone. If you can hold the sound, you're doing L or R, not ㄹ.

02Six Ways to Find the Sound

Different students respond to different cues. If one trick doesn't click, try the next. The goal is the same quick tap — just a different way in.

1The "butter / water" trick

Say butter, water, ladder, better the way Americans say them quickly — the "tt" or "dd" in the middle. That tap IS Korean ㄹ.

Works for: most American English speakers who use a "flap T". Some learners pronounce these words with a hard "t" — for them, skip to trick 2 or 3.

2The "ladder rung" image

Imagine your tongue tip touching a rung of a ladder and immediately bouncing off. Your tongue should feel light, not pressed.

Works for: visual / kinesthetic learners. Great when the butter trick doesn't land.

3The Spanish "pero" trick

If you've heard Spanish — the single 'r' in pero ("but"), caro ("expensive"), cero ("zero") — that's the same tap.

Works for: anyone exposed to Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, or Japanese. Especially good if "butter" gets pronounced as "but-ter".

4The drum tap

Say "la la la la" softly and slowly. Now say it faster and lighter, like a fingertip tapping a desk: "la-la-la-la". That speed = ㄹ.

Works for: when you want a no-comparison-needed reset. Just feel the tongue moving fast.

5The "no holding" rule

Try to hold the sound: "lllll". If you can — that's L, not ㄹ. ㄹ is impossible to hold. Try again, this time make it impossible to sustain.

Works for: students who can hear the difference but their tongue defaults to L. Forces the bounce.

6The "mirror" method

Practice in front of a mirror. Open your mouth slightly. Watch your tongue — it should briefly appear at the alveolar ridge and disappear, like a fish jumping out of water.

Works for: anyone. Especially powerful when no other trick has clicked yet.

03Position Matters — ㄹ Changes Slightly

Korean ㄹ has three sound flavors depending on where it sits in a word. The good news: native speakers do this automatically once the basic tap is there.

At the start

— sounds closer to L
라면 (ramyeon, ramen)
로봇 (robot)
라디오 (radio)

Between vowels

— quick tap (flap)
사람 (saram, person)
오리 (ori, duck)
마리아 (maria)

At the end (받침)

— softer L, tongue stays
살 (life, flesh)
말 (word, horse)
달 (moon)
💡 Reassuring fact The "between vowels" position is where the quick-tap sound is most distinct from English L and R. This is where mispronunciation is most noticeable to Korean ears — and the most rewarding to fix. Focus practice here.

05The Daily Drill

Two minutes a day beats one long session a week. Read aloud, slowly first, then faster.

Warm-up
라 랴 러 려 로 료 루 류 르 리
ra · rya · reo · ryeo · ro · ryo · ru · ryu · reu · ri
Repeat slowly 3 times, then faster 3 times.
Between vowels — the hardest spot
아라 어러 오로 우루 으르 이리
a-ra · eo-reo · o-ro · u-ru · eu-reu · i-ri
Feel the tap. Don't press. Don't curl.
Real words — try in front of a mirror
사람 오리 머리 다리 우리
saram · ori · meori · dari · uri
Watch your tongue. Tap, bounce, gone.

06Bonusvowels ㅗ vs. ㅜ

A different challenge — same idea. ㅗ and ㅜ are both rounded, but ㅗ is "oh" and ㅜ is "oo". Confusing them can completely change a word.

💡 Tip for ㅗ vs. ㅜ ㅗ — lips rounded but slightly looser, like saying "oh" in surprise.
ㅜ — lips tight and pushed forward, like saying "oo" while puckering. Try holding a chopstick lightly between your lips for ㅜ — it shouldn't fall.
