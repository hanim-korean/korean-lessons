02Six Ways to Find the Brief-Touch Action

These cues are only for ㄹ in a syllable onset or between vowels. They help students find the brief tongue movement without calling it an English R or L.

1The "butter / water" trick

Say butter, water, ladder, or better quickly in American English. The middle sound can help you feel a similar one-touch tongue motion. Copy the movement—not the English letter.

Works for: most American English speakers who use a "flap T". Some learners pronounce these words with a hard "t" — for them, skip to trick 2 or 3.

2The "ladder rung" image

Imagine your tongue tip touching a rung of a ladder and immediately bouncing off. Your tongue should feel light, not pressed.

Works for: visual / kinesthetic learners. Great when the butter trick doesn't land.

3The Spanish "pero" trick

If you know Spanish, the single consonant in pero, caro, or cero can help you feel a similar brief contact. Use it as a movement cue, not as the name of ㄹ.

Works for: anyone exposed to Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, or Japanese. Especially good if "butter" gets pronounced as "but-ter".

4The drum tap

Silently place the tongue tip near the ridge. Make one tiny touch as light as a fingertip tapping a desk, then release into the vowel. Keep the tongue relaxed—no English letter name needed.

Works for: when comparison words create interference. Focus only on the physical movement.

5The "one touch" rule

For the brief-touch action, count exactly one tongue contact: touch—release. Stop after one. This rule does not apply to 받침 ㄹ, where the tongue stays.

Works for: students who press too long in 라면 or 사람. Keeps the two tongue actions from being confused.

6The "mirror" method

Practice 라면 or 사람 in front of a mirror. The tongue tip should appear at the ridge for an instant and disappear. Then compare 말: the tip stays in place.

Works for: anyone. The contrast between 사람 and 말 makes the two actions visible.

03Position Changes the Tongue Action—Not ㄹ's Identity

Keep the same ㄹ target at the firm alveolar ridge, a little farther back from the upper front teeth. Depending on the syllable position, either make one brief contact or keep the tip there while air passes at the sides.

At the start

— brief contact, then release
라면 (ramyeon, ramen)
로봇 (robot)
라디오 (radio)

Between vowels

— one light touch while moving through
사람 (saram, person)
오리 (ori, duck)
마리아 (maria)

At the end (받침)

— tongue stays; air passes at the sides
살 (life, flesh)
말 (word, horse)
달 (moon)
💡 The teaching shortcut Do not ask, “Is this R or L?” Ask, “One brief touch, or stay and side-flow?” That question sends the student's tongue to the right action without activating an English sound category.

05The Daily Drill

Two minutes a day beats one long session a week. Read aloud, slowly first, then faster.

💡 Tip for ㅗ vs. ㅜ ㅗ — lips rounded but slightly looser, like saying "oh" in surprise.
ㅜ — lips tight and pushed forward, like saying "oo" while puckering. Try holding a chopstick lightly between your lips for ㅜ — it shouldn't fall.
